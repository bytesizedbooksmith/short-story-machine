# Scenes And What Characters Can Know

## What A Scene Is

A scene is one continuous stretch of story: one time and place, one point of view. Someone wants something, runs into resistance, and something is different by the end.

If nothing changes, it isn't a scene yet. Give it a change, merge it with another scene, or cut it.

## What Causes A New Scene

Start a new scene when any of these happens:

- **Time jumps.** We skip time instead of living through it.
- **The place changes with a break.** Walking down a lane mid-conversation is the same scene. Arriving somewhere after a gap is a new one.
- **The point of view changes.** A new head means a new scene.
- **The scene's question is settled and a new one begins.** This usually comes with one of the above.

These don't start a new scene on their own:

- Someone new walks in.
- The mood turns.
- The scene is getting long. Length alone is never a reason to split.
- A brief memory. It belongs to the scene it interrupts.

A longer flashback, with its own time, place and change, can be its own scene or can sit inside one. Decide on the scene card, so its words are planned for.

## What Characters Can Know

The point-of-view character knows only what they have:

- **Seen, heard or experienced** themselves, by this moment in the story.
- **Been told.** It may be wrong, and it counts as what they were told, not as fact.
- **Worked out** from what's in front of them. It's a guess until confirmed.
- **Learned from their past**, such as their job, their history, or the people they know.

They don't know:

- **Anyone else's private thoughts or feelings.** They only see what people say and do. "Her mouth tightened" is theirs to see. "She was ashamed" is only their guess, so it should read as one.
- **Anything out of their sight and hearing,** until someone tells them or they find evidence.
- **What's coming.** No "little did she know," and no foreshadowing that only the plan could supply.
- **Anything just because it's in the plan.** Later twists and secrets stay hidden until the story reveals them.

Timing matters too. A character can't act on something before the scene where they learn it, so each card separates what they know at the start from what they learn during the scene.

The reader learns only what the point-of-view character notices.

**This doesn't mean neutral narration.** Staying inside one head makes the voice stronger. The character can judge, guess, misread people and hold opinions. Being wrong is allowed, and often more interesting than being careful.
