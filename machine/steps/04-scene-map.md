# Step 4 — Scene Map

**Goal:** a card for each scene, so drafting has a clear path.

**Fills in:** the plan's **Scene Map** section.

## Do This

1. You draft the scene map from the Shape; don't make the author write it. Read [../guides/scenes.md](../guides/scenes.md) and let it decide where scenes begin and end. As a rough check, expect one scene per 1,000–1,500 words of target length (a 3,000-word story is usually 2–3 scenes; a 10,000-word story, 7–10). Each scene's word target is a guide, so give it enough room to be written fully.
2. Each scene card has:
   - **Purpose:** what this scene does for the story, in one line.
   - **Point of view:** whose head we're in.
   - **When and where:** the time (or how long after the last scene) and the place.
   - **Reader experience:** what the reader should feel or enjoy here (tension, a laugh, dread, relief), in a few words.
   - **What happens:** three to five bullet points.
   - **Supporting moments:** any side bit (a background customer, a phone call, a running joke), labeled by its job: *atmosphere*, *character beat*, *setup* or *subplot*. Only a subplot gets its own problem and solution. Note where attention returns to the main character.
   - **What changes:** how things are different at the end of the scene.
   - **Knows / doesn't know:** what the point-of-view character knows at the **start** of the scene, what they learn during it, and anything important they don't know yet. Follow the rule in the scene guide.
3. Check the **timeline**. If the story has a deadline or a countdown, make sure the events can actually fit in the time given. ("The show starts in six minutes" can't hold three conversations and a costume change.)
4. Check that every person who matters is **introduced before they're needed**. Then add to the plan's **Fixed Facts**: set times (a train, a deadline), key places, and what recurring objects and documents are, so one thing doesn't turn into another (the "day book" isn't sometimes the "ledger"). Anyone new in the scene map gets their facts too.
5. Show the author the map as a short list (scene number, purpose, one line of what happens). Ask if anything's missing, in the wrong order, or unnecessary. They don't need to check every detail.
6. **Choose the opening.** Read [../guides/hooks.md](../guides/hooks.md). Then offer the author **three genuinely different openings** for Scene 1, each as a sample first line plus one sentence on what's happening. Use different ways in from the guide (for example the arrival, the magic misbehaving, mid-problem). Mark your recommendation. None of them should open on preparing, arranging or waiting.
7. Put their choice at the top of Scene 1's card as **Opening hook:** and make the card's first bullet match it. Background the hook doesn't need moves later in the scene or out of it.

**Done when:** the author is happy with the order and has chosen an opening. Update `PROGRESS.md`, then go straight to Step 5. Don't wait for them.
