# Step 8 — ✋ Opening Sample

**Goal:** get the opening right before writing the rest. A weak opening repeated across a whole story is the most expensive mistake to fix.

**Creates:** `writing/scenes/scene-01.md` and `reviews/opening-challenge.md`.

## Do This

1. Run the **Drafter** ([../roles/drafter.md](../roles/drafter.md)) on Scene 1. Give it the brief, the Scene 1 packet, [../guides/hooks.md](../guides/hooks.md) and [../guides/scenes.md](../guides/scenes.md) only.
2. Run the **Challenger** with the **Opening** lens on the draft. Give it the draft, the brief, `writing/idea.md` (not the plan) and [../guides/hooks.md](../guides/hooks.md).
3. Fix the supported findings (two rounds at most). Save the fixed version as the current `scene-01.md`; the first draft goes to `archive/`.
4. Ask the author to read Scene 1. Tell them where the file is and offer to paste it into the chat if that's easier to read. Be plain about the checks: they test the hook and whether the scene follows the brief, not whether the voice is right. That's the author's call, so don't call the scene ready or good on the strength of a passed check. Ask them four things:
   - Does the opening hook you?
   - Does it sound like the story you planned?
   - Anything about the voice you'd change (too formal, too chatty, too thin, too much description)?
   - Does the dialogue sound like these people? Is any exchange flat or too back-and-forth?
5. Turn their answers into changes:
   - **Fix this scene:** revise Scene 1 and show them the result.
   - **Applies to the whole story** (a voice note like "less description," "more contractions"): add it to the brief, and ask whether it should go in their pen name's style sheet for future stories too.
   - **They share an example they like** (another opening, a passage from a book): say in one or two lines *what makes it work* (for example "the client's request is the first line of dialogue") and apply that. Don't turn the example into a list of things not to copy; banning its details steers the draft away from what the author liked.
6. Repeat until they're happy with Scene 1.
7. **If the author turns down the opening twice, stop patching.** Small fixes to an opening that isn't working rarely make it work. Instead, reread [../guides/hooks.md](../guides/hooks.md) and offer three **genuinely different** first pages (about 150 words each), each using a different way in. Ask which direction to take, or what they like from each. Then redraft Scene 1 from that direction, updating its card and packet. If the new opening changes the approved plan, say what changes and get a quick yes.
8. **At the same point, offer the way out:** they can edit Scene 1 themselves, or bring a version they like better from anywhere. If they do, keep their prose. Repair only continuity and point-of-view problems, list every change, and treat the result as the standard. Don't rewrite it into your own voice.

**Done when:** the author says the opening works. Save that version as `writing/scenes/scene-01.md`; it's now the **voice example** for every later scene. Write their approval in `PROGRESS.md`, tell them how many scenes you'll draft next, then go to Step 9.
